1958

The US created the Advanced Research Projects Agency (ARPA) as a response to Sputnik. It funded important computer and networking research.

1961

Leonard Kleinrock published his work on packet-switching networks, which provided an important theoretical foundation for computer communication.

1964

Paul Baran developed ideas for decentralized, resilient communication networks, including packet switching.

1965

The first wide-area computer connection was established between a computer in Massachusetts and another one in California, over a dedicated telephone line.

1968

ARPA developed specifications for a packet-switched research network and began selecting the technology and contractors needed to build it.

1971

Ray Tomlinson developed email and introduced the @ convention for user addresses.

1973

Vint Cerf and Bob Kahn began developing mechanisms to connect independent networks together.

1974

The TCP concept was published. This was the establishing foundation for TCP/IP.

1978

TCP split into TCP (Transmission Control Protocol) and IP (Internet Protocol).

1983

ARPANET adopted TCP/IP.

1984

DNS was introduced, to translate human-readable names into network addresses.

1986

The National Science Foundation (NSF) established NSFNET, an academic network.

1988

IRC was created.

1989

Governments started to lift restrictions on the internet. The internet was no longer an academic concept or uniquely government research.

1990

ARPANET was shut down.

1991

Commercial internet services expanded and became increasingly accessible.

1995

NSFNET backbone retired, and commercial network providers took over the internet.

Late 1990s

ISPs, dial-up access, networking equipment, and consumer PCs brought internet connectivity to millions of households.

2000s

DSL, cable, and other broadband technologies replaced dial-up for many users.

2007–2010

Mobile networks appeared, increasing internet access to almost everywhere.

2010s

Cloud computing, data centers, content-delivery networks, and high-speed fiber became fundamental components of internet infrastructure.

Present

Internet connectivity increasingly extends to machines, sensors, vehicles, industrial systems, and low-latency applications.

Key Technologies

Packet Switching

The concept of packet switching is a fundamental theory of the internet. It consists of dividing data into small packets which are forwarded through a network from one side to another.

TCP/IP

The core networking protocols which define inter-device and inter-network communication.

DNS

It aims to transform human-readable names (URLs) into network addresses.
